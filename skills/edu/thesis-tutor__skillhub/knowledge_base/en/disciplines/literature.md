# Literature - Thesis Writing Guide

## Overview
Literary thesis writing emphasizes textual analysis, theoretical interpretation, and critical engagement with literary works. The core assessment criteria are: analytical depth, theoretical sophistication, textual evidence, and original interpretation.

## Research Types

### 1. Close Reading
- **Features**: Detailed analysis of specific texts
- **Structure**: Text Selection → Analysis Framework → Close Reading → Interpretation → Conclusion
- **Key Points**: Attention to language, form, and meaning

### 2. Comparative Analysis
- **Features**: Compare two or more literary works
- **Structure**: Works Selection → Comparative Framework → Analysis → Synthesis → Conclusion
- **Key Points**: Systematic comparison, meaningful connections

### 3. Theoretical Application
- **Features**: Apply literary theory to texts
- **Structure**: Theory Overview → Text Selection → Application → Findings → Implications
- **Key Points**: Theoretical understanding, rigorous application

### 4. Historical Contextualization
- **Features**: Study literature in historical context
- **Structure**: Historical Background → Text Analysis → Context-Text Relationship → Significance
- **Key Points**: Historical accuracy, cultural understanding

## Chapter Structure

### Chapter 1: Introduction (8-12 pages)
1. Literary problem or question
2. Texts under consideration
3. Theoretical framework
4. Research objectives
5. Thesis structure

### Chapter 2: Literature Review (15-25 pages)
1. Previous scholarship on the texts
2. Theoretical approaches used
3. Critical debates
4. Research gaps

### Chapter 3: Theoretical Framework (10-15 pages)
1. Theory overview
2. Key concepts and terminology
3. Application methodology
4. Justification for theory choice

### Chapter 4: Analysis (25-40 pages)
1. Text 1 analysis
2. Text 2 analysis (if comparative)
3. Thematic exploration
4. Formal analysis
5. Intertextual connections

### Chapter 5: Discussion (10-15 pages)
1. Synthesis of findings
2. Contribution to literary criticism
3. Broader implications
4. Limitations

### Chapter 6: Conclusion (3-5 pages)
1. Summary of key insights
2. Original contribution
3. Future research directions

## Methodology

### Analytical Approaches
- **Formalism/New Criticism**: Focus on text itself
- **Structuralism**: Underlying structures and codes
- **Deconstruction**: Dismantling binary oppositions
- **New Historicism**: Text and historical context
- **Postcolonialism**: Power, empire, resistance
- **Feminism/Gender Studies**: Gender representation
- **Psychoanalytic criticism**: Unconscious desires
- **Ecocriticism**: Environment and nature

### Textual Analysis
- **Close reading**: Line-by-line analysis
- **Rhetorical analysis**: Language and persuasion
- **Narrative analysis**: Story and discourse
- **Stylistic analysis**: Language and form

### Research Methods
- **Archival research**: Manuscripts, letters, reviews
- **Digital humanities**: Text mining, corpus analysis
- **Reception studies**: Reader responses

## Common Issues

1. **Weak thesis**: No clear argument or interpretation
2. **Insufficient textual evidence**: Claims not supported by text
3. **Theory misapplication**: Superficial or incorrect use of theory
4. **Poor structure**: Lack of logical progression
5. **Overreliance on critics**: Too much summary, too little analysis

## Writing Standards

### MLA Formatting
- MLA 9th edition citation style
- Works Cited page
- In-text citations
- Proper quotation formatting

### Literary Analysis
- Present tense for discussing texts
- Close textual evidence
- Clear analytical framework
- Original interpretation

### Critical Engagement
- Engage with existing scholarship
- Acknowledge different interpretations
- Support claims with evidence
- Maintain scholarly tone

## Citation Format
- MLA 9th Edition (standard for literature)

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Is the thesis clearly stated?
- [ ] Is the theoretical framework appropriate?
- [ ] Is textual evidence sufficient?
- [ ] Is the analysis original?
- [ ] Is the argument logically structured?
- [ ] Is the writing polished and engaging?
