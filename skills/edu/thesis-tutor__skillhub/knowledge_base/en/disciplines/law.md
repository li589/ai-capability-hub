# Law - Thesis Writing Guide

## Overview
Legal thesis writing emphasizes doctrinal analysis, normative reasoning, and practical implications. The core assessment criteria are: legal question significance, analytical rigor, doctrinal accuracy, and contribution to legal scholarship.

## Research Types

### 1. Doctrinal Research
- **Features**: Analyze and synthesize legal rules and principles
- **Structure**: Legal Question → Case Analysis → Statutory Interpretation → Doctrine → Conclusion
- **Key Points**: Comprehensive case review, statutory interpretation methods

### 2. Comparative Research
- **Features**: Compare legal systems across jurisdictions
- **Structure**: Research Question → Jurisdiction A → Jurisdiction B → Comparison → Lessons
- **Key Points**: Systematic comparison, contextual understanding

### 3. Empirical Legal Research
- **Features**: Study law in action using social science methods
- **Structure**: Research Question → Methodology → Data Collection → Analysis → Implications
- **Key Points**: Valid methodology, representative sample

### 4. Interdisciplinary Research
- **Features**: Apply insights from economics, philosophy, or sociology to law
- **Structure**: Legal Problem → Theoretical Framework → Analysis → Reform Proposals
- **Key Points**: Theoretical integration, practical relevance

## Chapter Structure

### Chapter 1: Introduction (8-12 pages)
1. Legal problem or controversy
2. Research questions and objectives
3. Significance of the study
4. Methodology overview
5. Thesis structure

### Chapter 2: Literature Review (15-20 pages)
1. Historical development
2. Theoretical frameworks
3. Current scholarly debate
4. Research gaps

### Chapter 3: Legal Framework (15-20 pages)
1. Constitutional provisions
2. Statutory framework
3. Judicial interpretation
4. International instruments (if applicable)

### Chapter 4: Analysis (20-30 pages)
1. Doctrinal analysis
2. Case law examination
3. Policy considerations
4. Comparative perspectives

### Chapter 5: Discussion (10-15 pages)
1. Synthesis of findings
2. Implications for law reform
3. Practical recommendations
4. Limitations

### Chapter 6: Conclusion (3-5 pages)
1. Summary of key findings
2. Contribution to legal scholarship
3. Final reflections

## Methodology

### Doctrinal Methods
- **Case analysis**: Ratio decidendi, obiter dicta
- **Statutory interpretation**: Textual, purposive, historical
- **Treatise analysis**: Scholarly commentary

### Comparative Methods
- **Functional comparison**: Same problem, different solutions
- **Structural comparison**: System-level differences
- **Micro-comparison**: Specific rules comparison

### Empirical Methods
- **Surveys**: Judicial behavior, public attitudes
- **Interviews**: Stakeholder perspectives
- **Quantitative analysis**: Statistical patterns

## Common Issues

1. **Insufficient case analysis**: Limited scope of cases reviewed
2. **Weak statutory interpretation**: Failure to apply interpretive methods
3. **Poor structure**: Lack of logical flow between sections
4. **Overreliance on secondary sources**: Insufficient primary source analysis
5. **Jurisdictional confusion**: Mixing legal systems without clarity

## Writing Standards

### Legal Citation
- Use consistent citation format
- Include pinpoint references
- Distinguish primary and secondary sources
- Follow jurisdictional conventions

### Legal Terminology
- Use precise legal terms
- Define specialized vocabulary
- Avoid colloquialisms
- Maintain formal register

### Argumentation
- Clear thesis statement
- Logical structure
- Evidence-based reasoning
- Consider counterarguments

## Citation Format
- OSCOLA (Oxford Standard for Citation of Legal Authorities)
- Bluebook (US)
- AGLC (Australian Guide to Legal Citation)
- McGill Guide (Canadian)

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Is the legal question clearly defined?
- [ ] Is the doctrinal analysis comprehensive?
- [ ] Are primary sources adequately examined?
- [ ] Is the argument logical and persuasive?
- [ ] Are policy implications addressed?
- [ ] Is the writing clear and precise?
