# Psychology - Thesis Writing Guide

## Overview
Psychology thesis writing emphasizes empirical research, theoretical grounding, and methodological rigor. The core assessment criteria are: research question significance, theoretical contribution, methodological soundness, and practical implications.

## Research Types

### 1. Experimental Research
- **Features**: Manipulate variables to establish causality
- **Structure**: Introduction → Method → Results → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Random assignment, control conditions, manipulation checks

### 2. Correlational Research
- **Features**: Examine relationships between variables
- **Structure**: Introduction → Method → Results → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Appropriate measures, statistical power

### 3. Longitudinal Research
- **Features**: Study changes over time
- **Structure**: Introduction → Method → Results → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Attrition, cohort effects, practice effects

### 4. Qualitative Research
- **Features**: Explore subjective experiences
- **Structure**: Introduction → Method → Findings → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Trustworthiness, reflexivity, saturation

### 5. Meta-analysis
- **Features**: Quantitatively synthesize existing research
- **Structure**: Introduction → Method → Results → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Inclusion criteria, effect size calculation, heterogeneity

## Chapter Structure

### Chapter 1: Introduction (10-15 pages)
1. Psychological problem or phenomenon
2. Literature review
3. Theoretical framework
4. Research questions and hypotheses
5. Significance of the study

### Chapter 2: Literature Review (20-30 pages)
1. Theoretical foundations
2. Empirical evidence
3. Moderators and mediators
4. Research gaps

### Chapter 3: Methodology (15-25 pages)
1. Participants
2. Measures and instruments
3. Procedure
4. Data analysis plan
5. Ethical considerations

### Chapter 4: Results (15-25 pages)
1. Preliminary analyses
2. Descriptive statistics
3. Main analyses
4. Additional analyses

### Chapter 5: Discussion (15-20 pages)
1. Summary of findings
2. Theoretical implications
3. Practical implications
4. Limitations
5. Future directions

### Chapter 6: Conclusion (3-5 pages)
1. Key contributions
2. Final thoughts

## Methodology

### Research Design
- **Between-subjects**: Different participants per condition
- **Within-subjects**: Same participants across conditions
- **Mixed design**: Combination of both
- **Factorial design**: Multiple independent variables

### Data Collection
- **Surveys**: Self-report measures
- **Behavioral measures**: Reaction time, accuracy
- **Physiological measures**: EEG, fMRI, cortisol
- **Observations**: Behavioral coding
- **Interviews**: Structured, semi-structured

### Analysis Methods
- **ANOVA**: Comparing group means
- **Regression**: Predicting outcomes
- **Factor analysis**: Identifying latent constructs
- **SEM**: Testing complex models
- **Multilevel modeling**: Nested data
- **Bayesian analysis**: Prior knowledge integration

### Common Measures
- **Personality**: Big Five, MBTI
- **Intelligence**: WAIS, Raven's
- **Mood**: PANAS, BDI, STAI
- **Cognition**: Working memory, attention tasks

## Common Issues

1. **Demand characteristics**: Participant expectations
2. **Experimenter bias**: Unintentional influence
3. **Sample bias**: WEIRD samples (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic)
4. **Replication crisis**: P-hacking, publication bias
5. **Ethical issues**: Deception, debriefing

## Writing Standards

### APA Formatting
- APA 7th edition style
- Running head
- Abstract (250 words)
- Keywords
- Method section details

### Statistical Reporting
- Effect sizes (Cohen's d, η², r)
- Confidence intervals
- Exact p-values
- Power analysis

### Ethical Guidelines
- IRB approval
- Informed consent
- Debriefing procedures
- Confidentiality protection

## Citation Format
- APA 7th Edition (standard for psychology)

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Is the research question theoretically grounded?
- [ ] Is the method appropriate for the question?
- [ ] Are measures valid and reliable?
- [ ] Is the sample size adequate?
- [ ] Are statistical assumptions met?
- [ ] Are findings interpreted appropriately?
