# Medicine - Thesis Writing Guide

## Overview
Medical thesis writing emphasizes clinical relevance, methodological rigor, and evidence-based practice. The core assessment criteria are: clinical significance, study design quality, statistical validity, and translational potential.

## Research Types

### 1. Clinical Research
- **Features**: Study diseases, treatments, and patient outcomes
- **Structure**: Background → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusion
- **Key Points**: Patient safety, ethical approval, CONSORT guidelines

### 2. Epidemiological Research
- **Features**: Study disease distribution and determinants
- **Structure**: Introduction → Methods → Results → Discussion → Public Health Implications
- **Key Points**: Study design, confounding control, population representativeness

### 3. Laboratory Research
- **Features**: Basic science and translational research
- **Structure**: Introduction → Materials & Methods → Results → Discussion
- **Key Points**: Reproducibility, controls, validation

### 4. Systematic Review/Meta-analysis
- **Features**: Synthesize existing evidence
- **Structure**: Introduction → Methods → Results → Discussion → Conclusion
- **Key Points**: PRISMA guidelines, quality assessment, heterogeneity

## Chapter Structure

### Chapter 1: Introduction (8-12 pages)
1. Clinical problem and significance
2. Current knowledge and gaps
3. Research objectives and hypotheses
4. Thesis structure

### Chapter 2: Literature Review (20-30 pages)
1. Disease background
2. Pathophysiology
3. Current treatments
4. Evidence gaps

### Chapter 3: Methods (15-25 pages)
1. Study design
2. Participants and setting
3. Data collection
4. Outcome measures
5. Statistical analysis plan
6. Ethical considerations

### Chapter 4: Results (15-25 pages)
1. Participant characteristics
2. Primary outcomes
3. Secondary outcomes
4. Subgroup analyses
5. Adverse events

### Chapter 5: Discussion (15-20 pages)
1. Key findings
2. Comparison with literature
3. Clinical implications
4. Limitations
5. Future directions

### Chapter 6: Conclusion (3-5 pages)
1. Summary of findings
2. Clinical significance
3. Recommendations

## Methodology

### Study Designs
- **Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT)**: Gold standard for treatment efficacy
- **Cohort study**: Prospective or retrospective
- **Case-control study**: Rare diseases
- **Cross-sectional study**: Prevalence
- **Case series/report**: Novel observations

### Data Collection
- **Clinical data**: Medical records, examinations
- **Patient-reported outcomes**: Questionnaires
- **Laboratory data**: Biomarkers, imaging
- **Administrative data**: Hospital records

### Statistical Methods
- **Descriptive statistics**: Demographics, baseline characteristics
- **Inferential statistics**: Hypothesis testing, confidence intervals
- **Survival analysis**: Kaplan-Meier, Cox regression
- **Multivariable regression**: Adjusting for confounders
- **Diagnostic tests**: Sensitivity, specificity, ROC curves

### Reporting Guidelines
- **CONSORT**: Randomized trials
- **STROBE**: Observational studies
- **PRISMA**: Systematic reviews
- **STARD**: Diagnostic studies
- **CARE**: Case reports

## Common Issues

1. **Selection bias**: Non-representative sample
2. **Information bias**: Measurement error, recall bias
3. **Confounding**: Uncontrolled variables
4. **Small sample size**: Underpowered studies
5. **Multiple comparisons**: Type I error inflation

## Writing Standards

### Vancouver Style
- Numbered citations
- Author names: surname + initials
- Journal abbreviations
- Standard formatting

### Medical Terminology
- Use standard medical terms
- Define abbreviations at first use
- Include units for measurements
- Report confidence intervals and p-values

### Ethical Requirements
- IRB/Ethics committee approval
- Informed consent documentation
- Patient privacy protection
- Conflict of interest disclosure

## Citation Format
- Vancouver (standard for medical research)

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Is the clinical question clearly defined?
- [ ] Is the study design appropriate?
- [ ] Is the sample size adequate?
- [ ] Are confounders addressed?
- [ ] Are results clinically meaningful?
- [ ] Are limitations acknowledged?
