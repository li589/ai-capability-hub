# Agriculture Thesis Writing Guide

## Discipline Characteristics
- Emphasis on practical application, field experiments, yield improvement
- Strong focus on experimental design, statistical analysis, extension application
- Long research cycles (crop growth periods)
- Strong regional characteristics (climate, soil differences)

## Research Types

### 1. Field Trials
- **Applicable to**: Crop cultivation, variety breeding, plant protection
- **Key elements**:
  - Experimental design (randomized block, split-plot design)
  - Data collection (yield, quality, resistance)
  - Statistical analysis (ANOVA, multiple comparisons)
- **Thesis structure**: Objective → Materials → Methods → Results → Discussion

### 2. Laboratory Research
- **Applicable to**: Molecular biology, physiology and biochemistry, soil analysis
- **Key elements**:
  - Experimental methods (extraction, detection, analysis)
  - Data processing (statistical testing, charts)
  - Result verification (replication, controls)
- **Thesis structure**: Introduction → Materials → Methods → Results → Discussion

### 3. Survey Research
- **Applicable to**: Agricultural economics, rural development, agricultural policy
- **Key elements**:
  - Survey design (questionnaires, interviews)
  - Data collection (samples, sampling methods)
  - Analysis methods (descriptive statistics, regression analysis)
- **Thesis structure**: Background → Method → Survey → Analysis → Conclusion

## Discipline Subdivisions

### Crop Science
- **Crop cultivation**: Cropping systems, fertilization techniques, water management
- **Crop genetics and breeding**: Variety improvement, hybrid breeding, molecular breeding
- **Crop physiology**: Photosynthesis, nutrient metabolism, stress physiology

### Plant Protection
- **Plant pathology**: Disease diagnosis, control techniques, disease-resistant breeding
- **Agricultural entomology**: Pest control, natural enemy utilization, integrated management
- **Pesticide science**: Efficacy trials, residue analysis, safety evaluation

### Horticulture
- **Pomology**: Variety breeding, cultivation techniques, post-harvest handling
- **Olericulture**: Facility cultivation, quality regulation, safe production
- **Floriculture**: Variety resources, propagation techniques, flowering regulation

### Agricultural Resources and Environment
- **Soil science**: Soil fertility, soil improvement, soil pollution
- **Agricultural meteorology**: Climate resources, disaster warning, climate change
- **Agricultural ecology**: Ecological agriculture, circular agriculture, sustainable development

## Thesis Structure Template

### Field Trial Type
```
1. Introduction (background, objectives, significance)
2. Materials and Methods (experimental area, materials, design, measurements)
3. Results and Analysis (yield, quality, resistance, etc.)
4. Discussion (mechanisms, comparison, application)
5. Conclusion (main findings, recommendations)
```

### Laboratory Research Type
```
1. Introduction (research status, objectives)
2. Materials and Methods (materials, reagents, methods)
3. Results (data, charts)
4. Discussion (mechanisms, significance)
5. Conclusion (contributions, future directions)
```

## Writing Tips

### Field Trial Description
- Experimental location (latitude, longitude, altitude, climate)
- Soil conditions (type, fertility, pH)
- Experimental materials (variety, source, characteristics)
- Experimental design (treatments, repetitions, plot size)

### Data Presentation
- Yield data (kg/ha or t/ha)
- Quality indicators (protein, starch, etc.)
- Statistical analysis (F-test, LSD, Duncan)
- Chart standards (error bars, significance markers)

### Extension Value
- Economic benefit analysis
- Technical feasibility
- Regional adaptability
- Extension recommendations

## Common Issues

1. **Non-standard experimental design**: Lack of controls, insufficient repetitions
2. **Improper data processing**: Wrong statistical methods, messy significance markers
3. **Shallow discussion**: Only listing results, lack of mechanism analysis
4. **Unclear extension value**: Lack of economic benefit analysis
5. **Non-standard writing**: Inconsistent terminology, messy formatting

## Plagiarism Considerations
- Experimental method descriptions can be standardized
- Data analysis must be original
- Conclusions must be based on your own results
- Extension recommendations require your own thinking
