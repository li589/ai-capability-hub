# Bestseller analysis

Use this reference when the primary decision is to discover, validate, build, scale, repair, or retire a bestseller/hero product. Keep the scope to one primary platform/site and a declared category, price band, market, and period unless comparison is explicit.

## 1. Define the decision and the bestseller standard

Do not use one universal GMV threshold. Define the relevant outcome:

- **external opportunity**: a category/product pattern worth entering or benchmarking;
- **internal candidate**: a current SKU with repeatable growth potential;
- **validated hero**: a SKU that can scale with acceptable contribution, quality, and supply risk;
- **repair or retire**: a former hero whose economics, conversion, demand, or supply has deteriorated.

State the unit, currency, platform/site, period, ranking definition, and guardrails. Separate bestseller status from a temporary event spike.

## 2. Build six evidence layers

### Demand and momentum

Check category growth, rank movement, search/content interest, sales velocity, price-band fit, seasonality, and competitor density. External rankings are directional unless their method and coverage are known.

### Product-market fit

Check problem clarity, differentiation, review/VOC themes, use-case breadth, price-value perception, variant fit, and whether demand survives outside one creator, campaign, or keyword.

### Conversion quality

Trace exposure → product click/view → cart → paid order → retained order. Compare the candidate with its category/price-band peer set and isolate stock, traffic-source, event, and new-product effects.

### Economics

Use net revenue and contribution after discount, refund, platform/creator fees, fulfillment, advertising, and other variable costs. A high-GMV loss leader is not a validated hero unless its portfolio role and downstream value are evidenced.

### Traffic and content replicability

Separate organic, search, paid, affiliate/creator, live, and private-domain contribution. Test whether the winning hook, audience, placement, or creator mix can be repeated without rapidly worsening acquisition cost or refund quality.

### Supply and durability

Check sellable stock, stockout-adjusted velocity, lead time, MOQ, quality capacity, inventory days, replenishment cash, return reasons, rating/review stability, repeat purchase, and cannibalization.

## 3. Reconcile before scoring

Apply these checks:

1. normalize eligible selling days and mark stockouts;
2. compare like-for-like periods, traffic sources, campaign phases, and price bands;
3. separate paid price, platform subsidy, merchant discount, and creator commission;
4. distinguish estimated external sales from merchant settlement data;
5. isolate abnormal bulk orders, cancellations, refunds, and one-off creator spikes;
6. avoid combining metrics with incompatible attribution windows.

Lower confidence when these checks cannot be completed.

## 4. Classify the product

Use the smallest useful classification:

| Class | Evidence pattern | Default decision |
|---|---|---|
| Validated hero | durable demand + sound contribution + replicable traffic + supply safety | scale within explicit gates |
| Rising candidate | positive momentum with one or two unproven layers | run the cheapest discriminating test |
| Traffic-dependent spike | volume depends on one paid source, creator, event, or subsidy | diversify or cap exposure |
| Margin trap | strong GMV but weak contribution/refund quality | repair economics before scale |
| Supply-constrained winner | verified demand but stock/quality/cash is binding | fix replenishment and quality gate |
| False positive | rank, exposure, or gross sales looks strong but evidence does not survive normalization | stop or reframe the role |

Do not force a product into “hero” because it ranks first inside a small assortment.

## 5. Choose a decision view

- **Candidate discovery**: ranked dot plot or table across momentum, conversion, contribution, refund, and stock risk.
- **Scale decision**: demand–contribution bubble chart plus supply gate.
- **Rise/fall diagnosis**: traffic/conversion/economics/supply contribution bridge over time.
- **Content-commerce breakout**: content/creator → product click → paid order → retained order path, with concentration risk.
- **Portfolio consequence**: hero/profit/traffic/trial/exit matrix and cannibalization note.

Use exact tables when values and definitions matter more than visual pattern.

## 6. Produce the recommendation

Connect each material finding:

`evidence → interpretation → commercial impact → action → validation metric`

For scale, specify:

- eligible channel/audience and offer;
- inventory or quality capacity;
- budget and contribution floor;
- test window and review cadence;
- success threshold, warning threshold, and stop condition;
- owner and next replenishment/content/pricing decision.

For uncertainty, list no more than three ranked hypotheses. Name the evidence that would support or disprove each and the cheapest next test.

## 7. External intelligence rules

Read `research-tools.md` for current commercial intelligence, official platform pages, and other relevant sources. Record platform, list, category filter, statistical period, update date, ranking metric, and coverage limitation. Never treat account influence as product demand or estimated sales as audited merchant performance.

## 8. Minimum output

Include:

1. bestseller definition and evidence scope;
2. candidate/hero classification with confidence;
3. strongest demand, conversion, economics, traffic, and supply evidence;
4. the binding constraint or failure risk;
5. scale/validate/repair/stop decision;
6. owner, timing, metric, and gate for the next action.
