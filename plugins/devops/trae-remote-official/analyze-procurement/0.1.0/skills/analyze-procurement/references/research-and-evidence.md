# Research and evidence

Use this reference for current supplier, commodity, freight, tariff, regulation, and risk research.

## 1. Research brief

Define before browsing:

- decision and module;
- item/service/specification;
- geography and jurisdiction;
- evidence date or validity window;
- required facts;
- comparison unit and commercial basis;
- stop condition.

Search for the missing decision fact, not the whole industry.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. regulator, customs authority, sanctions/export-control authority, standards body, commodity exchange, or statistical authority;
2. supplier contract, official price sheet, technical documentation, SLA, security/compliance report, audited filing, and official status page;
3. carrier, port, terminal, broker, insurer, or recognized industry body;
4. specialist datasets and established market research with transparent methodology;
5. reputable reporting;
6. directories, aggregators, reseller pages, forums, and social posts as discovery leads only.

For supplier discovery, expand coverage using:

- industry association and certification registries;
- conference/exhibitor and standards participation lists;
- procurement frameworks and public-award records;
- partner/integration directories;
- patent, product, and technical documentation;
- import/export or facility data where lawful and suitable.

Do not treat a sponsored list, SEO ranking, or search result order as supplier quality.

## 3. Evidence card

Capture for each material external fact:

- claim;
- source and direct link;
- source type;
- published/effective/as-of date;
- accessed date;
- market/jurisdiction;
- unit, currency, tax, Incoterm, and tier where relevant;
- applicability limits;
- evidence status;
- conflict or corroboration note.

Use short quotations only when exact wording matters; otherwise paraphrase.

## 4. Topic-specific checks

### Supplier and AI/service pricing

- Distinguish list, contract, enterprise, committed-use, batch, cache, and promotional pricing.
- Record region, model/version, billing unit, context/rate limits, support, SLA, and effective date.
- Check whether capability claims come from supplier tests, independent tests, or the user's workload.
- Prefer workload evaluation over generic leaderboard rank for award decisions.

### Commodity and raw material

- Match grade/specification, delivery location, currency, tax, contract month, and lag.
- Separate exchange futures, spot assessment, supplier quote, and executed PO.
- Record basis, freight, processing/yield, and seasonal or policy drivers.
- Do not infer causal pass-through from visual correlation alone.

### Supplier risk

- Resolve the legal entity, parent, site, and manufacturing/subcontracting footprint.
- Check financial filings, regulatory actions, sanctions, recalls, certifications, cybersecurity incidents, and operational disruptions using primary sources.
- Time-stamp risk evidence; absence of public evidence is not evidence of absence.

### Freight and trade

- Match origin/destination, ports, equipment, service, validity, inclusion scope, free time, and schedule.
- Verify tariff, tax, product, origin, sanctions, and documentation with the relevant jurisdiction.
- Treat freight indices as market context, not an executable all-in quote.

## 5. Triangulation

Triangulate when a fact can reverse the decision:

- supplier price or capacity claim;
- mandatory certification or regulatory eligibility;
- material commodity benchmark;
- tariff classification or trade remedy;
- supplier distress or disruption;
- lane availability during a critical window.

Use at least one primary source and one independent corroborating source when feasible. If sources conflict, expose the conflict and convert it into a gate or scenario.

## 6. Stop conditions

Stop research when:

- the decision-critical facts are covered by current applicable evidence;
- at least two credible sources converge where triangulation is required;
- further search repeats the same claims without improving source quality;
- a missing internal specification, quantity, usage, or contract term becomes the binding constraint;
- the evidence cannot support more precision than a range.

Do not keep collecting suppliers merely to enlarge a longlist. Stop when the relevant market segments are represented and new candidates no longer change shortlist or RFx design.
