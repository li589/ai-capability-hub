# Supplier performance and risk

Use this module for scorecards, QBRs, SQE reviews, supplier development, allocation, audits, CAPA, capacity, continuity, and exit decisions.

## 1. Define the decision consequence

Choose the action the evaluation must support:

- qualify or disqualify;
- approve a site/item;
- increase, cap, or reduce allocation;
- initiate or escalate CAPA;
- audit or capacity-validate;
- place on watch;
- dual-source;
- suspend or exit.

Do not build a scorecard without an action policy.

## 2. Reconcile performance definitions

Align:

- supplier legal entity and site;
- item family and risk class;
- requested, promised, ship, and receipt dates;
- on-time tolerance;
- ordered versus received/in-full quantity;
- inspected versus received denominator;
- defect, complaint, escape, return, warranty, and severity;
- capacity nameplate, committed, demonstrated, available, and bottleneck;
- period, sites, and exclusions.

Preserve supplier disputes and internal data-quality exceptions.

## 3. Apply gates before scores

Typical gates:

- mandatory certification or audit;
- critical quality escape;
- product/safety/security failure;
- sanctions/trade/legal failure;
- unapproved process/site change;
- unresolved critical CAPA;
- inability to meet required capacity or recovery.

Represent gates as pass, fail, condition precedent, or unknown. Do not average them.

## 4. Score only comparable preferences

Use anchored criteria where possible:

- price/TCO against a declared baseline;
- OTD/OTIF under one definition;
- quality severity and rate;
- response and CAPA closure;
- service/engineering support;
- demonstrated capacity and surge;
- continuity and concentration;
- evidence completeness.

Run weight sensitivity. If a small weight change reverses the ranking, report the tie and use a pilot, audit, negotiation, or allocation portfolio rather than a false winner.

## 5. Test capacity and continuity

Inspect available evidence for:

- bottleneck equipment/process;
- demonstrated rate and yield;
- current utilization and committed load;
- labor, tooling, material, utility, and sub-tier constraints;
- changeover and ramp;
- surge and recovery time;
- alternate site/process;
- business continuity tests.

Do not ask the user for every item as a batch. Use visible evidence first, mark unsupported claims unknown, and request only the one item that blocks the current gate or allocation decision.

Use a scenario tied to the award quantity and peak period.

## 6. Recommend a control, not a rank

State:

- action and allocation;
- gate or CAPA;
- owner and due date;
- verification evidence;
- interim containment;
- fallback;
- review metric/date;
- stop or exit trigger.

Keep raw performance, score, risk exposure, and management action in separate views.
