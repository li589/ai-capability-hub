# International logistics and landed cost

Use this module for freight quote normalization, routing, container planning, Incoterm comparison, duties/taxes, lead time, and trade-compliance gates.

## 1. Test feasibility first

Before cost comparison, verify or gate:

- product and dangerous-goods status;
- HS classification status;
- origin and preference claim;
- import/export licensing and controls;
- sanctions/restricted-party screening ownership;
- product labeling, certification, and documentation;
- carrier/equipment/route acceptance;
- destination capability and delivery constraints.

Do not let a low quote outrank an infeasible lane.

## 2. Normalize freight quotes

Align:

- origin/destination and named places;
- port pair and service;
- mode, equipment, container, chargeable weight, or pallet basis;
- cargo weight, volume, dimensions, stackability, and temperature;
- base freight and surcharges;
- origin and destination charges;
- brokerage, insurance, duty, tax, and delivery;
- free time, detention/demurrage basis, and accessorial exclusions;
- sailing/flight schedule, cutoff, transit time, and validity;
- FX date and tax basis.

Create an inclusion matrix before totaling.

## 3. Build the load or container plan

Check:

- weight and volume utilization;
- floor load and center of gravity where relevant;
- pallet/pack dimensions and orientation;
- stacking, crush, temperature, segregation, and dangerous-goods constraints;
- legal payload and route limits;
- minimum billable quantity and partial-load break-even;
- number of containers/shipments and residual cargo.

Treat a geometric packing result as a candidate plan until operationally validated.

## 4. Compare total outcome

Show:

- expected all-in landed cost and landed unit cost;
- cash tax/duty timing and recoverability;
- door-to-door lead time and schedule frequency;
- inventory in transit;
- expected and tail accessorial cost;
- reliability/capacity evidence;
- compliance/document gates.

Use break-even volume/weight or delay cost when the preferred mode changes with shipment size or urgency.

## 5. Stress the lane

Test the binding disruption:

- rollover/capacity shortage;
- peak surcharge or spot-rate move;
- port/route disruption;
- customs hold or document error;
- demurrage/detention;
- FX move;
- late production missing cutoff.

Recommend a primary lane, fallback, booking trigger, quote-validity deadline, document owner, and stop condition.

## 6. Communicate legal uncertainty

State HS, origin, valuation, duty, tax, sanctions, and license conclusions as confirmed, provisional, assumed, or unknown. Route binding determinations to the responsible customs broker, legal, trade-compliance, or tax owner.
